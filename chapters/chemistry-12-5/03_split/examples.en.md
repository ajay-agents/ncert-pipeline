:::example{number="5.1" kind="example" id="ex_5.1" topic="Secondary valence from AgCl precipitation data"}
#### Example 5.1

:::prompt
On the basis of the following observations made with aqueous solutions, assign secondary valences to metals in the following compounds:

| Formula | Moles of AgCl precipitated per mole of the compounds with excess $\mathbf{A g N O}_{\mathbf{3}}$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| (i) $\mathrm{PdCl}_{2} .4 \mathrm{NH}_{3}$ | 2 |
| (ii) $\mathrm{NiCl}_{2} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ | 2 |
| (iii) $\mathrm{PtCl}_{4} .2 \mathrm{HCl}$ | 0 |
| (iv) $\mathrm{CoCl}_{3} .4 \mathrm{NH}_{3}$ | 1 |
| (v) $\mathrm{PtCl}_{2} .2 \mathrm{NH}_{3}$ | 0 |
:::

:::solution{label="Solution"}
(i) Secondary 4
(ii) Secondary 6
(iii) Secondary 6
(iv) Secondary 6
(v) Secondary 4
:::

:::example{number="5.4" kind="example" id="ex_5.4" topic="Absence of geometrical isomerism in tetrahedral complexes"}
#### Example 5.4

:::prompt
Why is geometrical isomerism not possible in tetrahedral complexes having two different types of unidentate ligands coordinated with the central metal ion?
:::

:::solution{label="Solution"}
Tetrahedral complexes do not show geometrical isomerism because the relative positions of the unidentate ligands attached to the central metal atom are the same with respect to each other.
:::

:::example{number="5.6" kind="example" id="ex_5.6" topic="Identifying the chiral (optically active) isomer"}
#### Example 5.6

:::prompt
Out of the following two coordination entities which is chiral (optically active)?
(a) cis- $\left[\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}(\mathrm{OX})_{2}\right]^{3-}$
(b) trans- $\left[\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}(\mathrm{ox})_{2}\right]^{3-}$
:::

:::solution{label="Solution"}
The two entities are represented as

(a) cis - $\left[\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}(\mathrm{ox})_{2}\right]^{3-}$

(b) trans $-\left[\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}(\mathrm{ox})_{2}\right]^{3-}$
Out of the two, (a) cis - $\left[\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}(\mathrm{ox})_{2}\right]^{3-}$ is chiral (optically active).
:::

:::example{number="5.7" kind="example" id="ex_5.7" topic="Predicting geometry from magnetic moment"}
#### Example 5.7

:::prompt
The spin only magnetic moment of $\left[\mathrm{MnBr}_{4}\right]^{2-}$ is 5.9 BM. Predict the geometry of the complex ion ?
:::

:::solution{label="Solution"}
Since the coordination number of $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ ion in the complex ion is 4, it will be either tetrahedral ( $s p^{3}$ hybridisation) or square planar ( $d s p^{2}$ hybridisation). But the fact that the magnetic moment of the complex ion is 5.9 BM, it should be tetrahedral in shape rather than square planar because of the presence of five unpaired electrons in the $d$ orbitals.
:::
